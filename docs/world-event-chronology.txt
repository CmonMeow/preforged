Shared World event chronology: integration design
2026-10-18; design only, not an implemented runtime guarantee

Problem and present limits

NPC Flare, NPC ordinary and NPC elemental deliveries retain an arrival deadline,
but resolve overdue deliveries against the processing tick's time and state.
Player ordinary projectiles also resolve at processing time. The independent
NPC flight queues solve ownership, capacity and action-blocking problems; they
do not establish arrival-time equivalence between fine and delayed ticks.

At a late tick, intervening regeneration, degeneration, protection expiration,
Blind, movement, equipment changes and end-heals can change a hit or its death
credit. Two overdue deliveries are currently visited by controller/family or
release order, not necessarily arrival order. A condition applied by an overdue
hit starts late. An earlier lethal hit can lose attribution to a later event.

Changing each resolver's now argument to shot.arrives is unsafe. Health, Energy,
conditions, control and movement may already have advanced beyond that deadline.
Movement history and expired effects may have been discarded. Never lower those
clocks, reconstruct past facts from current ones, or clamp an event to the latest
actor clock and call that chronological resolution.

Authority and evidence

The implementation must reuse current damage, protection, conditions, Energy,
Health, action-control, attack-outcome and lifetime authorities. It must not
create parallel resource pools or a second source of gameplay formulas.
Native skill rows supply activation/aftercast facts; authored projectile speeds,
hit tolerances, capacity and equal-time ordering remain private-server policies.
data/gw/ai/action-interval-evidence.txt explicitly distinguishes release from
arrival and recovery. Captures do not prove the proposed equal-time tie policy.

Provider inventory and concrete touchpoints

1. src/runtime/gw_world_simulation.h, Instance::tick:
   currently settles fields and movement/NPC conditions to now before the AI
   drivers, then drains projectile families and advances player resources.
   A shared barrier must precede these full-horizon operations.

2. advance_health_locked and advance_resource_event_locked in that file:
   advance_health recursively invokes Lightning, Fevered, Parasitic and field
   settlement before integrating Health. Existing nested full-horizon drains
   must become boundary-limited dispatch. Include linked NPC degeneration and
   linked caster benefits; actor-local ordering alone is insufficient.

3. Existing discrete providers:
   - gw_damage_fields.h: DamageFieldSchedule::next_deadline; advance currently
     sets its cursor to the requested horizon and eagerly drains all pulses.
     Add peek/dispatch-one semantics without advancing past another provider.
   - gw_lightning_strike.h / gw_lightning_surge.h: existing next_deadline
     authorities merged by settle_lightning_strikes_locked.
   - gw_parasitic_bond.h: next_deadline; settle_parasitic_returns_locked already
     limits a Health forecast by damage_fields_.next_deadline(). Extend this
     cooperation instead of adding another independent full-horizon drain.
   - settle_fevered_deadlines_locked and settle_arcane_returns_locked: include
     their deadline/end-reaction work and cross-actor consequences.

4. Released delivery providers:
   - gw_world_npc_flare.inl: resolve_npc_flare_releases_locked, immutable shots.
   - gw_world_npc_ordinary.inl: resolve_npc_ordinary_locked and event ledger.
   - gw_world_npc_elemental.inl / gw_npc_elemental_ai.h: immutable release ledger.
   - World resolver families: ordinary, generic projectiles, lightning, water,
     stone, javelins, Deathly Swarm and elemental projectiles.
   Each needs a nonmutating next-arrival/peek and exact-handle dispatch. Keep
   effect ownership in its existing ledger; the arbiter stores typed handles,
   source/target lifetime scopes and ordering metadata, never actor pointers.

5. Continuous boundaries:
   Health condition/regeneration expiry, health_crossing_end and passive-recovery
   changes; modifier-owned Energy expiry and first_zero; NPC equivalents.
   gw_overcast_settlement.h can return a settlement stopped at first zero, and
   gw_owned_energy_hex.h supplies observer/batch mechanics. Helpers alone are
   not runtime integration: Health must not cross that boundary first, and all
   zero-Energy observers must share the actual transition before continuation.

6. External mutation barriers:
   resource settlement/payment/gain, damage/healing, status/condition application
   and removal, equipment/armor changes, movement inputs, join/leave and revival
   must drain earlier relevant events before replacing authoritative facts.
   Cover actual Runtime-to-World calls, not only Instance::tick. Existing public
   setters that call settle_damage_fields_locked are useful audit starting points.

7. Geometry:
   healing_position_locked rejects times before last_move/steering_tick and
   returns a point, while callers often use the current surface plane. Preserve
   or materialize both point AND surface at each event boundary before consuming
   movement history. Include NPC routes and condition-modified movement budgets.

Required invariants

- One drain horizon; no integration or mutation may pass an unresolved earlier
  causal boundary. Per-family monotonically increasing clocks must agree with it.
- Pick the earliest time across providers. Equal times use documented phases
  consistent with existing expiry/zero/death rules, then a stable World insertion
  ordinal for otherwise independent events. Actor IDs and map iteration are not
  priority. Audit ties explicitly; do not silently choose a new retail rule.
- Integrate continuous state only to the chosen boundary, dispatch there, then
  recompute the earliest boundary because callbacks may create/remove events.
- Preflight required facts and publication capacity, then consume or mark the
  event in progress before callbacks. Reentry must not replay it. Revalidate
  lifetime scopes after cross-actor settlement and before committing a hit.
- A nested request during dispatch at t cannot advance another actor to later T.
  Use an active-dispatch time/phase guard and queue continuation, not a boolean
  guard that silently skips required work and lets the caller proceed to T.
- Source death does not invalidate already released NPC deliveries. Retirement
  does; a replacement lifetime never inherits hits or animation stops.
- Unknown/future observations are not misses. Preserve unresolved work and return
  an explicit incomplete result without advancing dependent state past it. An
  initial strict earliest-boundary barrier is safer than claiming arbitrary
  actors are independent despite area effects, drains and reactive callbacks.
  Diagnose unexpected blockers; reject unsupported admission where possible.
- Bound work/capacity. Exhausting a drain budget returns incomplete and preserves
  its frontier; it must not skip events or advance the frontier to the horizon.

Ordered implementation and first coherent runtime slice

1. Inventory timestamp-advancing public paths and provider queues. Specify event
   phases, handle lifetime, incomplete-result propagation and reentry contracts.
2. Add peek/dispatch-one adapters and a shared arbiter, backed by actual existing
   queues. Include existing timed-effect/continuous providers as ordering fences.
3. Extract continuous-only boundary advancement from recursive full-horizon
   Health/Energy/movement settlement. Integrate the zero-Energy observer dispatch
   here, preserving death cleanup and all affected actors' modifier removals.
4. Wire tick and public mutation/resource paths through the same barrier before
   enabling arrival-time dispatch. A wrapper used only by tick is incomplete.
5. Recommended first vertical slice: released NPC Flare and ordinary deliveries
   to players, interleaved with real player projectile, field, timer and resource
   boundaries on those same actors. Keep the other projectile providers as real
   adapters/fences, not invisible queues; an unported earlier event must prevent
   claiming the horizon settled. Do not enable this globally while ordinary
   input paths can bypass the barrier or competing queues remain invisible.
6. Expand exact dispatch across the remaining projectile families using the same
   contracts. Remove redundant eager drains only after their callers use the
   shared barrier. Keep AI choices once per outer tick; do not run target/skill
   selection once per internal event. Initially preserve actual release time
   for late windup completion; scheduling completion at its historical deadline
   requires a separate explicit completion provider and readiness contract.

Acceptance tests through real World APIs

Compare fine and delayed drains with the same timestamped input sequence:
- Reversed numeric source IDs, arrivals at 2000/3000: earliest lethal hit owns
  death; different family iteration orders cannot change the result.
- Arrival at 2500, protection expiration at 3000, end-heal at 3500: damage,
  survival, expiry reactions and attribution match fine stepping.
- Movement input at 3000 after arrival at 2500: the hit drains before history
  replacement. Cover a surface transition as well as a position change.
- Field pulse, player projectile and NPC Flare share a victim; add an Energy-zero
  transition before predicted Health death and verify all modifier observers.
- A callback queues a same-time event and removes another: each accepted event
  runs once; dead/retired actors do not receive later invalid state mutations.
- Equal-time ordering, incomplete future/unknown observations, work-budget
  exhaustion, replacement lifetimes and source death preserve the invariants.
- Existing aftercast, failed-cast, release-capacity and ordinary attack observers
  still run exactly once and retain their original authorities.

A standalone queue test is useful but insufficient. Completion requires these
World/resource/input paths and equivalence tests, not merely a scheduler helper.
